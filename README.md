## What “Japan CN2 GIA VPS” actually means

A Japan VPS and a Japan CN2 GIA VPS are not automatically the same thing.

A normal Japan VPS may use standard transit, local peering, or a mixture of carriers. That can work well for users in Japan and nearby regions, but performance toward mainland China may vary significantly by carrier, destination province, and time of day.

CN2 GIA refers to China Telecom’s **AS4809 Global Internet Access** network. BandwagonHost describes it as a premium route designed to provide more stable China-bound connectivity than ordinary ChinaNet or lower-tier CN2 GT transit. The provider also notes that CN2 GIA has limited capacity and higher transit costs, which is one reason these VPS plans are much more expensive than standard Japan hosting.

That does not mean every connection will have the same latency or that packet loss can never occur. Routing depends on:

- Your access carrier in mainland China
- The destination city and ISP
- The direction of traffic
- Current congestion
- International network changes
- Whether the application is sensitive to latency, packet loss, or bandwidth

In practical terms, a Japan CN2 GIA VPS is usually considered when the connection to China matters more than getting the lowest monthly VPS price.

## Bandwidth, transfer, and port speed are different

The plan table contains three separate network-related numbers:

- Monthly transfer allowance
- Link speed
- Network route

They answer different questions.

**Monthly transfer** is the amount of data included in the billing period. Osaka 40G and Tokyo 40G each list 500 GB per month. The 1280G plans list 8 TB per month.

**Link speed** is the maximum port or uplink rate shown by the product page. The Osaka plans list 1.5 Gbps, while Tokyo lists 1.2 Gbps. This does not mean every user will download at that speed. Actual throughput depends on the remote network, congestion, protocol, server workload, and traffic direction.

**Network route** describes the path and carriers used to reach China and other destinations. A high port speed with a poor route can still produce a disappointing result for China-bound traffic. Conversely, a premium route cannot overcome a slow application, weak server configuration, or a congested last-mile connection.

This is why “Japan VPS with 1 Gbps port” and “Japan CN2 GIA VPS” should not be treated as interchangeable search terms.

## How to test a Japan CN2 GIA VPS before moving production traffic

A short test period is more useful than relying on a generic “Japan VPS review.” Try to measure from the networks that your users actually use.

Check the following:

1. **Latency from multiple Chinese regions**
   Test from eastern, northern, southern, and western locations where possible. A result from one city does not represent the entire country.

2. **Different access carriers**
   China Telecom, China Unicom, and China Mobile may take different paths. Since the product page lists routes involving all three, testing more than one carrier is important.

3. **Packet loss during busy periods**
   Run tests at different times of day. A route that looks excellent at noon may behave differently in the evening.

4. **Upload and download in both directions**
   Many users only test download speed. Server-to-user and user-to-server performance can differ.

5. **Application-level performance**
   Ping and traceroute are useful, but they do not tell you whether your database, TLS setup, file delivery, or web application performs well.

6. **IPv4 and IPv6 separately**
   If your service advertises IPv6, confirm that the route and access behavior meet your needs. Do not assume IPv6 performance matches IPv4.

A reasonable evaluation should include real requests to your application, not only a single speed-test screenshot.
